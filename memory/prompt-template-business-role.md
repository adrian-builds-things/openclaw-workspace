# Prompt-Template: ROLE + Business Context

## Prompt

You are a [ROLE — e.g., senior business strategist / executive copywriter / operations consultant].

Business Context:
What my business does: [One clear sentence]
My target customer: [Who they are and their main problem]
My brand voice: [3 words — e.g., direct, confident, human]
What success looks like: [The outcome this piece of work needs to produce]

Task:
[Exactly what you need — be specific about the format, topic, and purpose]

Constraints:
Length: [Exact word count or range]
Tone: [Match brand voice — add any specifics]
Must include: [Non-negotiable elements]
Must avoid: [Phrases, approaches, or content to skip]
Output format: [Describe exactly how you want it structured — headers, paragraphs, bullets, subject line + body, etc.]

Thinking instruction:
Before you write, take 2-3 sentences to state your understanding of what I need and who I'm writing for. Then write the output.

---

## Beschreibung: How To Use This Prompt Template

Step 1: Copy the full template above. Don't trim it. The structure is the point.

Step 2: Fill in the ROLE with the expert most relevant to your task.
- "Senior business strategist" for planning work.
- "Executive copywriter" for client-facing content.
Be specific — Sonnet 4.6's improved instruction-following means the role shapes the entire output.

Step 3: Complete the Business Context section once and save it somewhere permanent.
Your business type, customer, and voice don't change much. This section gets pasted into every prompt you run.

Step 4: Write a precise Task description.
Don't say "write a good email."
Say "write a 150-word follow-up email to a prospect who went quiet after receiving a proposal three days ago."

Step 5: Fill in Constraints.
Sonnet 4.6 is significantly less prone to overengineering and meaningfully better at instruction following — which means when you set a word count, it actually respects it. Use that.

Step 6: Keep the Thinking Instruction at the bottom.
This is the feature that separates Sonnet 4.6 from what came before.
The model can pause and reason through a problem before generating a final response.
Asking it to state its understanding before writing activates that reasoning layer and stops it from charging in the wrong direction.
